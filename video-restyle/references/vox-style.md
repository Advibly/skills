# Template: vox-style

The explainer-desk editorial look. The subject is lifted out as a die-cut
sticker with a thick white outline and placed over a bright yellow field
of black and off-white Bauhaus geometry: circles, half-circles, grids,
checkerboards, triangles, diagonal stripes. It reads like a motion-graphics
explainer from a premium editorial channel: designed, confident, a little
playful, never messy.

**Pick it when**: the content is explanatory or opinion-with-structure
(breakdowns, how-it-works, "here's why", listicles, analysis) and the user
wants a designed editorial frame rather than podcast-pop's loud chaos.

**Subject treatment: cutout-real.** The person stays fully photoreal. Only
the world around them is replaced. Use the identity preservation block from
SKILL.md verbatim.

## Style block (verbatim in every segment prompt)

```
Style: the person is cut out of their original background as a die-cut
sticker with a thick clean white outline and a soft drop shadow, placed
over a bright yellow editorial background composed of flat black and
off-white geometric shapes: circles, half-circles, grids, checkerboards,
triangles and diagonal stripes, arranged like a designed magazine spread.
The person stays completely photoreal inside the sticker. The shapes drift
and rotate very slowly so the layout feels alive. A solid black caption bar
with bold white grotesque uppercase lettering sits in the lower third. The
whole frame reads as a premium editorial explainer graphic.
```

## Variation axes (rotate per segment)

**Axis 1: the geometric arrangement.** Rotate so no two adjacent segments
repeat.

1. **Scattered composition**: a balanced scatter of mixed shapes (a big
   circle, a small grid, two triangles, a checker strip) around the subject.
2. **Dominant circle**: one oversized black circle behind the subject's
   head like a halo, a thin off-white grid filling one side.
3. **Diagonal checker band**: a bold checkerboard band cutting diagonally
   across the frame behind the subject, small shapes in the corners.
4. **Stacked column**: a column of stacked shapes (circle, square, half
   circle) running down one side, generous empty yellow on the other.

**Axis 2: the field color.** The dominant field rotates: signal yellow with
black and off-white shapes, off-white field with yellow and black shapes,
black field with yellow and off-white shapes (the inverted segment, use
once per video), mustard with cream and black.

**Axis 3: editorial annotations.** On segments with concrete `topics`, one
annotation appears: a thin hand-drawn circle around the subject's head, a
small arrow pointing at them, an index number ("01", "02") in a corner, or
a thin underline sweeping beneath the caption. One per segment maximum.

## Energy moves

- **Punch-in**: on the strongest emphasis word, the framing punches in
  tighter on the person's face, then eases back. Use on one or two segments.
- **Layout swap**: on a phrase turn, the shapes slide off and the next
  arrangement slides in, a designed rearrangement rather than a hard cut.
- **Inversion**: exactly one segment per video takes the black field from
  axis 2; place it on the video's biggest claim.

## Beat vocabulary (anchor these to words)

- **Shape snap-in**: on the opening word, the geometric shapes snap into
  place one after another building the layout.
- **Circle land**: exactly on the word that names the topic, the dominant
  circle lands behind the subject's head, or the annotation arrow points in.
- **Checker sweep**: on a mid-segment word, the checker band sweeps across
  to its position.
- **Caption bar slide**: on the segment's final strong word, the black
  caption bar slides in and locks, and the layout settles for the cut.

A packed 8-10s segment uses two or three of these; the emphasis word gets the
punch-in or the biggest layout move.

## Caption treatment

The black caption bar from the style block. Per-segment line:

```
The black caption bar reads exactly "WHY THIS ACTUALLY WORKS", spelled
exactly, every word correct, bold white grotesque uppercase lettering,
anchored in the lower third.
```

3-6 words. The bar keeps the same position and height across every segment.

## Example beat timeline

Segment spoken line: "There are three reasons this works. The first one is
the one nobody talks about." (10s, emphasis on "nobody")

```
The segment runs this beat timeline over the spoken words:
- It opens on the word "There" as flat black and off-white shapes snap into
  a scattered composition across a signal-yellow field, a big circle, a
  small grid and two triangles settling around the photoreal sticker of the
  person, then drifting slowly.
- Exactly on the word "three" a black index number "01" lands in the upper
  corner.
- Exactly on the word "nobody" the framing punches in tighter on the
  person's face, and the black caption bar slides in reading exactly "THE
  REASON NOBODY MENTIONS", spelled exactly, every word correct, bold white
  grotesque uppercase lettering in the lower third, then the layout
  settles. The person's voice and the room's sound continue naturally.
```

## Failure modes

- **The subject got stylized**: the identity block got crowded. Shorten the
  shape inventory, keep "completely photoreal inside the sticker" adjacent
  to the person lines, re-roll.
- **No white outline / subject blends in**: strengthen to "thick clean
  white die-cut sticker outline all the way around the person".
- **The layout turned cluttered or random**: name fewer shapes and say
  "arranged like a designed magazine spread, balanced, with breathing
  room".
- **Colors drifted off the system**: restate the three-color discipline:
  "only the yellow field, flat black, and off-white, nothing else".
- **Caption misspelled**: re-roll the same prompt first; then shorten.
- **The frame barely moves**: restore the slow drift line and anchor the
  shape snap-ins and caption slide to exact words.
