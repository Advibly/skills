# Template: cardboard-cutout

The handmade craft edit. The subject is ripped out of their original footage
with a rough torn cardboard edge, like a photo torn from a shipping box, and
placed on a corrugated cardboard world: visible flutes, kraft-brown panels,
strips of packing tape, marker scribbles and rubber-stamp marks. Stop-motion
maker energy. Tactile, DIY, zero-gloss.

**Pick it when**: the content is maker, indie, behind-the-scenes,
unboxing-adjacent, or wants an anti-polish handmade feel (bootstrapped
founder updates, product teardowns, craft brands, "we built this" stories).

**Subject treatment: cutout-real.** The person stays fully photoreal. Only
the world around them is replaced. Use the identity preservation block from
SKILL.md verbatim.

## Style block (verbatim in every segment prompt)

```
Style: the person is torn out of their original background as a cutout with
a rough torn cardboard edge all the way around, raw fiber visible along the
rip, with a soft drop shadow, placed on a corrugated cardboard background
with visible flutes and kraft-brown texture. The person stays completely
photoreal inside the cutout. Strips of translucent packing tape hold the
cutout at its corners. Hand-drawn black marker doodles and stamped ink
marks decorate the cardboard around them. Everything moves with a subtle
handmade stop-motion wobble. A strip of masking tape with handwritten black
marker capitals sits in the lower third as the caption. The whole frame
reads as a handmade cardboard craft edit.
```

## Variation axes (rotate per segment)

**Axis 1: the cardboard scene.** Rotate so no two adjacent segments repeat.

1. **Plain sheet**: one large corrugated sheet, flutes running horizontal,
   a torn strip revealing a second layer along one edge.
2. **Layered panels**: two or three overlapping cardboard panels at slight
   angles, taped where they meet, casting thin shadows.
3. **Box interior**: the inside of an open shipping box, flaps at the frame
   edges, the subject centered in the well.
4. **Kraft collage**: torn pieces of kraft paper and cardboard taped into a
   collage, a strip of bubble wrap along one edge.

**Axis 2: the accent set.** One marking style per segment, never repeated on
adjacent segments: black marker doodles (arrows, underlines, stars), red
rubber-stamp marks (a circle, "FRAGILE"-style stamped shapes without brand
names), white paint-pen scribbles, blue ballpoint hatching.

**Axis 3: craft props.** On segments with concrete `topics`, one cardboard
prop cut from the same material pops in beside the subject: a cardboard
arrow, a cut-out star, a small cardboard chart with a marker line, a
cardboard lightbulb. One per segment maximum.

## Energy moves

- **Panel slap-in**: on a phrase turn, the next cardboard panel slaps into
  place behind the subject with a small settle wobble, revealing the next
  scene in the rotation.
- **Punch-in**: on the strongest emphasis word, the framing punches in
  tighter on the person's face, the wobble briefly stronger, then eases
  back.
- **Tear reveal**: once per video, on a big claim, a strip of the cardboard
  background tears away to reveal a fresh layer in a different kraft tone.

## Beat vocabulary (anchor these to words)

- **Tape-down**: on the opening word, the tape strips press down over the
  cutout's corners and the scene settles.
- **Prop pop**: exactly on the word that names a topic, the cardboard prop
  pops in beside the subject and wobbles to rest.
- **Stamp thump**: on a mid-segment emphasis word, a stamped ink mark
  thumps onto the cardboard near the subject.
- **Marker sweep**: a hand-drawn marker arrow or underline draws itself
  toward the subject on the word it supports.
- **Caption press**: on the segment's final strong word, the masking-tape
  caption strip presses on and the frame settles for the cut.

A packed 8-10s segment uses two or three of these; the emphasis word gets
the punch-in, stamp thump, or tear reveal.

## Caption treatment

The masking-tape strip from the style block. Per-segment line:

```
The masking-tape caption strip reads exactly "BUILT IN A GARAGE", spelled
exactly, every word correct, handwritten black marker capitals on the tape,
anchored in the lower third.
```

3-6 words. The tape strip stays in the same screen position across
segments; its angle can vary a few degrees.

## Example beat timeline

Segment spoken line: "We had no budget, so we built the first version
ourselves. It worked." (10s, emphasis on "worked")

```
The segment runs this beat timeline over the spoken words:
- It opens on the word "We" as translucent tape strips press down over the
  corners of the torn-edge photoreal cutout, on a plain corrugated sheet
  with horizontal flutes, black marker doodles drifting with a subtle
  stop-motion wobble.
- Exactly on the word "built" a cardboard arrow cut from the same material
  pops in pointing at the person and wobbles to rest.
- Exactly on the word "worked" a red ink stamp thumps onto the cardboard
  beside them, and the masking-tape caption strip presses on reading
  exactly "IT ACTUALLY WORKED", spelled exactly, every word correct,
  handwritten black marker capitals, anchored in the lower third, then the
  frame settles. The person's voice and the room's sound continue
  naturally.
```

## Failure modes

- **The subject got stylized** (cartooned or paper-textured): the identity
  block got crowded. Keep "completely photoreal inside the cutout" adjacent
  to the person lines, shorten the scene description, re-roll.
- **Clean die-cut edge instead of torn cardboard**: strengthen to "a rough
  torn cardboard edge with raw fiber visible along the rip, never a smooth
  outline".
- **The wobble became jitter or shake**: say "a subtle handmade stop-motion
  wobble, small and gentle" and re-roll.
- **Brand names or shipping logos appeared on the cardboard**: say "the
  cardboard is plain with no printed logos or brand markings"; stamped
  shapes stay generic.
- **Caption illegible** (marker handwriting is a spelling risk): re-roll
  first, then shorten to 3-4 words.
- **The frame barely moves**: restore the wobble line and anchor the
  prop pops, stamp thumps and caption press to exact words.
