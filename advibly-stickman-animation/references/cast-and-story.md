# Story toolkit: invent the concept (the OPEN layer)

This is the open, creative half of the skill. The LOOK is locked in `storyboard-prompts.md` and
`animate-prompts.md`; the STORY is yours to invent for each brand. This file gives you the
principles, a menu of devices and structures, and one fully worked example so you can see the
method. **It is a toolbox, not a template.** Nothing here is a required arc. If two different
brands come out with the same beats and a different product, you have used this file wrong.

## What "stickman style" locks (and what it leaves free)

**Locked (always true, from the LOOK files):** flat 2D vector stick figures, uniform black
linework, a pure white void, the two-accent color system, the comic-VFX vocabulary, snappy limited
animation, SFX-only clips with an external voiceover. That is the style. It does not change.

**Free (you invent per brand):** the concept and hook, who the characters are, what the setting is,
how many beats, the structure and arc, the jokes, the metaphor (if any), the pacing, the emotional
tone. A stickman ad can be a punchy problem-to-solution pitch, a silly one-joke gag, a calm
slice-of-life, an absurd exaggeration, a mock instructional, or a pure vibe piece. The style
carries all of them.

## The two-accent color system (part of the locked look)

Near-monochrome, with meaning carried by color:

| Color | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **White (#FFFFFF)** | the void + the figure's fill | background everywhere |
| **Black** | all linework and features | every outline |
| **{BRAND_COLOR}** | the product and its positive energy | the hero color, the brand's primary hex |
| **Gray** | an optional negative / "problem" element | use ONLY if your story has a problem to embody; not required |

`{BRAND_COLOR}` is read from the brand's primary color in Phase 1 (default a bold value if none).
Keep it disciplined: it appears only on the product and its positive energy. Gray is optional and
only for a problem device if you invent one. Everything else is black on white.

## Be the creative director (do this first)

Before any beats, decide the IDEA. Pull from the brand brief (audience, pain points, benefits,
voice-of-customer lines, proof, objections) and ask:

- What is the single sharpest thing to make this audience feel or realize about this product?
- What is the funniest, most literal, or most surprising way to show that in flat stick figures?
- What everyday moment, exaggeration, or metaphor lands it fastest?
- What is the hook in the first 2 to 3 seconds? Stickman comedy lives or dies on the opening beat.

Write a one-line concept before writing beats. If you cannot say the idea in a sentence, it is not
sharp enough yet.

## Device menu (pick what fits, or invent your own)

Ways to dramatize the point. Reach for one that serves THIS idea; do not default to the first.

- **Anthropomorphized problem or feeling** as a character: a gremlin, a blob, a shadow, a
  literal rain cloud, a nagging little devil, a tangled knot. Strong when the pain is an internal
  state. (This is the device in the worked example. It is one option, not the house style.)
- **Anthropomorphized benefit or product** as a helpful character or sidekick.
- **Metaphor made literal:** the abstract benefit drawn as a physical thing (a weight lifting off,
  chains breaking, a fog clearing, a wall the figure walks through, a tangled cord untangling).
- **Before / after** split or transformation on one figure.
- **Expectation vs reality** two-panel or two-beat contrast.
- **Running gag / escalation:** the same beat repeats and escalates until the product breaks the loop.
- **Day in the life / slice of life:** small true moments the product improves.
- **Mock demo / instructional:** deadpan "how it works" with comic diagrams.
- **Countdown / list:** "3 reasons," "5 signs you need this," each a quick stick gag.
- **Personified objects or numbers:** stats, reviews, or competitors drawn as stick characters.
- **Pure absurdity / non-sequitur** that loops back to the product.

## Structure menu (pick a shape, size it to the idea)

- **Problem to solution (DR):** setup the pain, deepen it, introduce the product, show relief,
  close on the product. Classic and reliable; 4 to 9 beats.
- **Gag with payoff:** setup, escalation, punchline that is the product. 3 to 5 beats.
- **Transformation:** one figure changes because of the product. 3 to 6 beats.
- **List / countdown:** a framing beat plus one beat per item plus a close.
- **Testimonial-in-stick:** a stick "person" tells a tiny story to camera.
- **Vibe / brand feeling:** loosely connected beats that build a mood and land on the logo.

Beat count follows the idea and the length, not a rule. A tight 20 to 30s gag might be 3 to 4
beats; a fuller 60 to 70s story might be 8 to 9. Each beat is one clip (~5 to 10s). The only near
universal rule is: earn a setup and deliver a payoff.

## Design the cast and world for THIS story

Lock, in precise redraw-able words, only what your story actually uses:

- **The protagonist stick figure:** the neutral look, plus any state variants your story needs
  (tired, excited, confused, whatever). The base figure is always the same simple construction
  (circle head, flat white fill, dot eyes, single-line limbs, uniform stroke).
- **Any recurring element you invented** (a mascot, an anthropomorphized problem or benefit, a
  signature prop): describe its silhouette exactly. This is what drifts across beats, so the wording
  must be reused verbatim.
- **The product** as a flat 2D {BRAND_COLOR} cartoon icon with the real label/logo text copied.
- **The world:** the handful of flat line-art settings and props the story needs.
- **The narrator voice** and the accent {BRAND_COLOR}.

## Narration voice and timing

- **One narrator voice for the whole ad**, matched to the tone your story wants. Voice map: `leo`
  (deep, authoritative announcer), `rex` (confident, punchy CTA), `ara` (warm, friendly), `sal`
  (smooth, calm; good for wellness or a gentle piece), `eve` (energetic, upbeat, playful). Pick one
  and keep it.
- **Write each beat's line to time**, ~2.5 to 3 words per second, so no clip rides silent. Rough
  guide: a 6s beat holds ~15 to 18 words; an 8s beat ~20 to 24; a 10s beat ~26 to 30. Generate one
  VO clip per beat (see `audio-and-gotchas.md` for why and how to place them).
- **The lines read as one script** straight through. Write them to flow, even though they are
  rendered and placed per beat.
- **A character can have a short comic line** in a different voice if your story wants it; fold it in
  or render it separately and drop it at that beat's offset.

## Worked example (ILLUSTRATION ONLY, do not reuse this arc)

To show the method end to end, here is the full concept the style was reverse-engineered from. It is
**one execution** of the style for one energy-drink brand. Study how the pieces fit; do not port
this arc onto the next brand.

- **Concept (one line):** "The afternoon slump is a gray gremlin cloud that rides your head all day
  until the drink blasts it off."
- **Device:** anthropomorphized problem (a gray "mood cloud" of fatigue that latches onto the head).
- **Structure:** problem-to-solution DR, 9 beats.
- **Accent:** the brand's red, used only on the can and its energy.
- **Beats:** (1) asleep, the cloud latches on; (2) groggy morning routine, cloud as gloomy hair;
  (3) commute, the cloud rains on them; (4) drained at the desk, cloud grinning; (5) the can glows
  on a shelf; (6) reach and hesitate; (7) drink, white flash, energy floods the figure, the cloud is
  blasted off; (8) back at the desk, fast and glowing; (9) hero can with a POW burst, cloud left
  crying. Music switched from a tense drone to an upbeat beat at beat 7.

Now notice what makes it work as a *method*, independent of the specific content: a clear device
carried consistently, a sharp first-beat hook, one problem embodied and escalated, a satisfying
turn, and a product that resolves the exact tension the opening set up. **Apply that method to a
fresh idea; do not apply this bed-cloud-desk content.** A sleep brand, a SaaS tool, a snack, and a
skincare line should each get a different concept, device, setting, and arc.

## Cost shape (present before firing, wait for confirmation)

Roughly, per ad: one still per beat (gpt-image-2, anchored on the first style plate), one clip per
beat (`gemini-omni-flash`, ~5 to 10s each), one voiceover render per beat line (~0.03 credits per
1000 characters each), and one or two music beds (0.3 credits each). Stitch and the local mix are
free. Scale the still and clip counts to your beat list, present the total, and wait for explicit
confirmation before generating.
