# Ad Angles Reference

Shot-by-shot breakdowns for all five preset angles. Each includes visual direction, dialogue patterns, product reference notes, and which shots are creator-only vs product-forward. Shots run 8 seconds each on Gemini Omni Flash (10 if the dialogue needs the room).

Any shot can be reframed as B-roll (hands-only close-up, product on a surface, environment detail) when the creator's face adds nothing; problem-agitation and transformation beats are the usual candidates. B-roll dialogue plays as voice-over, and the shot's storyboard frame is generated without the creator reference.

---

## Angle 1 - Testimonial (Default)

Best for: supplements, skincare, any product with a specific result claim.
Structure: 5 shots, 8 seconds each, ~40 seconds total.

**Shot 1 - Hook**
Environment: Kitchen, bathroom, or any clean neutral interior
Action: Creator holds product toward camera
Product: Yes, held with both hands, label visible
Dialogue pattern: Single result claim. "I've been [doing X] for [timeframe] and [specific result]."
Lighting: Soft window light
Notes: This is your scroll-stopper. One claim. No setup. Product visible immediately.

**Shot 2 - Problem agitation**
Environment: Bedroom (morning), desk (work), or any relatable "before" context
Action: Creator without product; tired, overwhelmed, or frustrated expression
Product: No
Dialogue pattern: Describe the before state specifically. "I used to [specific pain]. Before [time/action], I was already [negative state]."
Lighting: Slightly dimmer; morning light through curtains, overhead only
Notes: Make the pain specific. "Tired all the time" is weak. "Hitting a wall at 2pm every single day" is strong.

**Shot 3 - Discovery**
Environment: Kitchen, bathroom, or where they would realistically find the product
Action: Creator opens, picks up, or interacts with product
Product: Yes, opening/rotating/examining
Dialogue pattern: Natural, slightly skeptical intro to product. "A friend told me about [product/category] and I figured [low-stakes reason to try it]."
Lighting: Warm, natural
Notes: Product name appears here for the first time. Don't pitch, introduce naturally.

**Shot 4 - Transformation**
Environment: Gym, outdoors, work, anywhere active
Action: Creator in motion or looking/feeling visibly different
Product: No
Dialogue pattern: Specific result at a specific time. "By [timeframe] I noticed [specific change]. [Timeframe 2]: [bigger specific change]."
Lighting: Natural, active environment appropriate
Notes: "I feel better" is weak. "Waking up before my alarm" is strong. Specificity = believability.

**Shot 5 - CTA**
Environment: Back to Shot 1 environment; consistency signals confidence
Action: Creator holds product toward camera, slight smile
Product: Yes, held toward camera, label visible
Dialogue pattern: One action, remove friction. "If you're [brief pain restate], [try it for timeframe]. [Link/access note]. [Risk removal: money back / nothing to lose]."
Lighting: Match Shot 1
Notes: Never "shop now." "Link is below" or "I'll link it" reads as UGC. "Shop now" reads as ad.

## Angle 2 - Car / On-the-Go

Best for: supplements, snacks, energy products, convenience-angle products, anything portable.
Structure: 4 shots, 8 seconds each, ~30 seconds total.
Key environment: Car interior throughout; consistency is the visual hook.

**Shot 1 - Hook in car**
Environment: Driver's seat, residential area visible through window
Action: Creator holds product toward camera
Product: Yes, product visible, appropriate for the category
Dialogue pattern: Convenience setup. "Okay, I used to [messy/complicated old routine] in my car."
Lighting: Soft overcast daylight through windshield / warm afternoon side light

**Shot 2 - Problem demo**
Environment: Same car, passenger seat or bag visible
Action: Creator mimes the old routine: mixing, measuring, spilling, etc.
Product: No (or old product if applicable)
Dialogue pattern: Describe the chaos specifically. "[Action] rolling everywhere, [mess] going everywhere."
Lighting: Match Shot 1

**Shot 3 - Product demo**
Environment: Same car
Action: Creator opens product or holds it up (hold-and-show, no consumption on camera)
Product: Yes, interaction with product
Dialogue pattern: Simple, punchy contrast. "Now I just [simple action]. No [pain 1], no [pain 2], no [pain 3]."
Lighting: Match Shot 1

**Shot 4 - Social proof / volume**
Environment: Same car, bag or purse visible on passenger seat
Action: Creator pulls multiple units out of bag, holds them toward camera
Product: Yes, multiple units
Dialogue pattern: "I have [number] of these in my bag at all times. [Simple why]."
Lighting: Match Shot 1
Notes: The "I stock up" shot is a strong conversion signal. It implies the creator bought multiple, not gifted one.

## Angle 3 - Unboxing

Best for: apparel, accessories, skincare, anything with packaging worth revealing.
Structure: 3-4 shots, 8 seconds each, ~30 seconds total.

**Shot 1 - Reveal**
Environment: Kitchen table, desk, or clean surface with the box/package in front of creator
Action: Creator opens the box, reacts to seeing the product
Product: Packaging visible first, then product emerges
Dialogue pattern: Anticipation then reaction. "These finally came in." / "Okay, okay." / "Oh, these are beautiful."
Lighting: Natural overhead or window light, not studio

**Shot 2 - Close detail**
Environment: Same surface, product now out of box
Action: Creator holds product close to camera, rotates to show key details
Product: Yes, close-up, fills most of frame
Dialogue pattern: Specific detail callout. "[Feature they notice], I didn't even know it did that." / "[Texture / material / color] is actually [honest descriptor]."
Lighting: Match Shot 1
Notes: Specificity sells. "The quality is great" is weak. "The stitching is actually insane up close" is strong.

**Shot 3 - Wear / use**
Environment: Mirror, bathroom, or context-appropriate space
Action: Creator wearing or using the product, looking at themselves or camera
Product: On or in use
Dialogue pattern: First impression of use. "Okay this is [honest reaction]. I wasn't expecting [specific thing]."
Lighting: Natural

**Shot 4 - CTA (optional)**
Environment: Same as Shot 3
Action: Creator looking at camera, product in frame
Dialogue pattern: "I'll link this below. Just get it." / "You need this. Link is below."

## Angle 4 - Lifestyle Demo

Best for: apparel, home goods, drinkware, anything that fits into a daily routine.
Structure: 5 shots, 8 seconds each, ~40 seconds total.

**Shot 1 - Environment establish**
Environment: The lifestyle context: kitchen, gym, home office, outdoor space
Action: Creator in scene, product incidentally visible but not featured
Product: In background or held casually
Dialogue pattern: Context setup. "My morning routine has completely changed since I started using [product category]."
Notes: This shot establishes "this is a real life" before the product reveal.

**Shot 2 - Product intro**
Environment: Same or adjacent space
Action: Creator picks up, holds, or shows product
Product: Yes, product forward
Dialogue pattern: Natural intro. "This is the [product name]. I've been using it for [timeframe]."

**Shot 3 - Use case A**
Environment: In context: at desk, in kitchen, at gym, etc.
Action: Creator using product in the relevant context
Product: In use
Dialogue pattern: First benefit. "[Use case] is so much [better/easier/different] now."

**Shot 4 - Use case B**
Environment: Different context or same with different application
Action: Different use of same product
Product: In use
Dialogue pattern: Second benefit. "And then [second use case], which I wasn't even expecting."

**Shot 5 - Wrap / CTA**
Environment: Back to Shot 1 environment
Action: Creator holds product, direct to camera
Product: Yes, held toward camera
Dialogue pattern: "Honestly one of the best purchases I've made this year. Link is below."

## Angle 5 - Problem-Solution

Best for: any product that solves a specific, nameable problem. Especially effective for functional products: organizers, tools, ergonomic accessories, cleaning products.
Structure: 4 shots, 8 seconds each, ~30 seconds total.

**Shot 1 - Problem state**
Environment: Where the problem occurs: cluttered desk, messy drawer, uncomfortable workspace
Action: Creator in the problem situation, frustrated or resigned
Product: Not visible yet
Dialogue pattern: Name the problem with specificity. "I have been dealing with [specific problem] for [timeframe] and I genuinely thought it was just [resigned acceptance]."
Notes: The more specific the problem, the more qualified the audience. "Messy desk" is weak. "My charging cables were taking up an entire drawer" is strong.

**Shot 2 - Product reveal**
Environment: Same space but before the solution is installed/used
Action: Creator holds product toward camera, first reveal
Product: Yes, clear reveal, packaging or product itself
Dialogue pattern: Introduce without overselling. "A friend sent me [product name]. I was like, okay, this is a stretch."

**Shot 3 - Demo**
Environment: Same space, product in use
Action: Creator demonstrates the solution in action
Product: In use, solving the problem
Dialogue pattern: Walk through what it does. "You [action with product], and then [result]. That's it. That's the whole thing."
Notes: Keep the demo to one action. Two actions in one shot gets confusing. Show the single clearest benefit.

**Shot 4 - After state / CTA**
Environment: Same space, now with the problem solved
Action: Creator shows the improved state, holds product toward camera
Product: Yes, held or visible in the improved environment
Dialogue pattern: Result + CTA. "[Specific result], [timeframe] later. I'll link this below. You need it."
